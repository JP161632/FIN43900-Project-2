# Fresh 30-month exercise - edited presentation and claim checks

The original completed student text is preserved unchanged in `evidence/fresh-locked-exercise/student-reconciliation.md`. This derivative summarizes it and separately qualifies causal wording; it does not rewrite the frozen prediction or claim a different original response.

| Metric | 36-month window | 30-month window |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net cumulative return | 24.85% | 25.89% |
| Annualized realized net volatility | 29.61% | 28.69% |
| Month-end maximum drawdown | -28.14% | -28.14% |
| Largest quarterly target movement | 20 percentage points | 20 percentage points |

The student predicted realized volatility in the 28%-29% range and target movement above 20 points. The volatility prediction was supported on this case; the target-movement prediction was not. The recommendation remains no capital deployment and further paper research only.

The realized volatility reduction is about 0.92 **percentage points**, and the cumulative return improvement is about 1.03 **percentage points**. These are absolute differences, not relative percentage improvements. Drawdown is unchanged to the reported precision. The 30-month case's maximum start-of-month security weight is 95.26%, so the concentration concern remains.

The original explanation that a 30-month window had enough "data cushion" to prevent aggressive turnover is an interpretation, not an isolated causal finding. The maximum target-change metric alone is not turnover. Recorded purchases-plus-sales relative to NAV, including initial deployment, sum to 2.3704 versus 2.5342 in the 36-month case. Those observed differences do not establish why they occurred or that they will persist.

Shortening a rolling window removes the oldest six observations at each evaluation date; it does not simply remove late-2021 observations from every estimation window. Nor does lower historical estimation risk mechanically imply lower subsequent realized risk. The original prediction remains unchanged even where its reasoning is qualified after the result.

The same-sample replay does not establish prospective superiority or universal unsuitability of every four-stock strategy. This particular process remains unsuitable for deployment under its disclosed evidence and decision policy. The student-written decision, acknowledgement of the failed target-change prediction, and no-change explanation are retained as actual evidence.
